## 2. The Economic Spacetime Metric

Unlike v3.0 (which used 2D surface distance), v4.0 uses the **Schwarzschild-like Metric** to calculate the true cost of moving resources (flux) between planetary blocks.

### 2.1 The Cost Function ($ds^2$)
The economic distance between events is defined by the line element:

$$ ds^2 = g_{\mu\nu} dx^\mu dx^\nu = -(1 + 2\Phi/c^2) c^2 dt^2 + (1 - 2\Phi/c^2) d\mathbf{x}^2 $$

*   **$\Phi(x)$**: Economic Potential at coordinate $x$. High $\Phi$ (deep gravity well) implies high market density but high exit costs (energy tariffs).
*   **$d\mathbf{x}$**: Spatial displacement (transport).
*   **$dt$**: Time elapsed (opportunity cost).

**Rule:** Economic flux follows the **Geodesic** that minimizes $\int ds$.

## 4. Spacetime Circuit Theory (G-Cart 4D)

Governance is redefined as **Impedance Matching** on a circuit with significant lag.

### 4.1 Lag as Inductance ($L$)
Communication delay $\Delta t$ due to distance $d$ and light speed $c$ is modeled as Inductance:

$$ L_{econ} \propto \frac{d}{c} \cdot \mu_{complexity} $$

*   **$\mu_{complexity}$**: Organizational permeability (magnetic permeability equivalent).

### 4.2 The Governance Equation (LRC Model)
The injection of budget/resources $I(t)$ into a remote block is governed by:

$$ V(t) = L \frac{dI}{dt} + RI(t) + \frac{1}{C} \int I(t) dt $$

*   **$L \frac{dI}{dt}$ (Inertial Term):** The **Back Electromotive Force** (Social Rejection). Rapid policy changes (high $dI/dt$) in high-lag zones ($L$) cause massive rejection voltage.
*   **$RI(t)$ (Dissipative Term):** Administrative friction and transport entropy.
*   **$\frac{1}{C} \int I dt$ (Capacitive Term):** Accumulation of wealth in the block's infrastructure ($C$).

### 4.3 Impedance Matching Condition
To maximize power transfer (work) and minimize reflection (waste), the source impedance $Z_S$ must match the load impedance $Z_L$:

$$ Z_S = Z_L^* \quad (\text{Conjugate Matching}) $$
